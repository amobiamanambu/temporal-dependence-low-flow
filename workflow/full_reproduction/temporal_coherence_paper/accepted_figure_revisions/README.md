### Required Figure 11 caption content

The final caption must retain the information removed from the map panels so
the cleaner design does not weaken reproducibility or interpretation:

- Panels a–d represent 30-, 60-, 90-, and 120-day forecast windows.
- Calibration bias is basin-mean forecast probability minus observed held-out
  Q10-onset event frequency, expressed in percentage points.
- Red means predicted low-flow risk was too low, blue means predicted risk was
  too high, and near-white values indicate little bias.
- Mapped values are clipped at ±35 percentage points only for color scaling;
  archived source-table values remain unclipped.
- All four panels contain the same 3,402 basins with estimable occurrence
  forecasts.
- Median biases at 30, 60, 90, and 120 days were 0.0, −0.5, −1.0, and −1.7
  percentage points, respectively.
- The corresponding percentages of basins with predicted risk too low were
  50.0%, 52.9%, 54.4%, and 56.0%.
- The maps show descriptive basin averages rather than site-level significance
  tests.

Recommended final caption wording:

> Spatial calibration bias of basin-level Q10-onset occurrence forecasts for
> (a) 30-, (b) 60-, (c) 90-, and (d) 120-day forecast windows. Bias is the
> basin-mean forecast probability minus the observed held-out event frequency;
> red indicates predicted low-flow risk that was too low, blue indicates risk
> that was too high, and near-white values are close to zero. Mapped colors are
> clipped at ±35 percentage points to preserve visual resolution, while
> archived source-table values remain unclipped. Across the 3,402 basins,
> median bias was 0.0, −0.5, −1.0, and −1.7 percentage points at 30, 60, 90,
> and 120 days, respectively; the corresponding fractions with predicted risk
> too low were 50.0%, 52.9%, 54.4%, and 56.0%. These are descriptive basin
> averages, not site-level significance tests.
